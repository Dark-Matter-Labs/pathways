A first sample

As DM enters its next ten years, we are beginning with a growing set of questions emerging from the conversations we are part of.

These ten are a first sample for year one. They offer starting points for shared inquiry and experimentation.

We expect to add further questions and to deepen, connect and revise these as we learn.

01–10 / Year one

Starting questions

Ten emerging inquiries and what each opens up.
Emerging inquirySeedling questionWhat it opens up
01
Species Nation
 Seedling question

What would it mean for a species, such as whales, to and exercise collective agency across human borders?

Exploring this term

An inquiry into political standing and collective agency for a species, including representation, resources and treaty relationships across human jurisdictions.

 What it opens up

Political membership, representation, territory, resources and treaty relationships. How might a species shape the conditions of its own future?
02
Inquiry Native Organising
 Seedling question

How could we organise around shared inquiry and evolving commitments, with at the core?

Exploring this term

Shared inquiry becomes part of how an organisation allocates attention and resources, holds commitments and revises its assumptions.

 What it opens up

A thesis of organising encompassing authority, accountability, institutional memory and resource allocation. How can institutions learn and revise their assumptions while sustaining responsibility?
03
A Conscious Economy Beyond Labour
 Seedling question

What would an economy centred on , intrinsic motivation and freedom to contribute look like beyond labour?

Exploring this term

Life and belonging have standing beyond productive usefulness. The inquiry includes care, imagination, meaning and the freedom to contribute.

 What it opens up

Material security, shared wealth and participation that enable care, imagination, embodied intelligence and meaning, with belonging independent of productive usefulness.
04
Three Degree City
 Seedling question

What would a city need to become to sustain life in a world ?

Exploring this term

A scenario used as a material design condition. The question asks how the city's coupled systems would need to change to sustain life.

 What it opens up

The hard systems reality of habitation: heat, water, food, energy, buildings, public health and municipal economics, examined through their interdependencies.
05
The Future of MoneyA New Monetary Lab
 Seedling question

How could securitised claims on verified resources and future productive value, , support present-day liquidity and new forms of money?

Exploring this term

The African Monetary Lab concept separates enforceable backing from redemption liquidity. Guarantees or insurance cover defined risks; liquid reserves or facilities support payments when due.

African Monetary Lab concept paper ↗
 What it opens up

Resource-backed currencies; claims on future production, restoration and receivables; third-party guarantees and liquidity facilities. The proof, enforceable rights, issuance, redemption and governance needed to make those claims usable as money while retaining public value and monetary sovereignty.
06
Free EverythingEverything is self-owning
 Seedling question

What would a world look like if and people entered into relationships of use, care and obligation with it?

Exploring this term

People relate to entities through use, care and obligation. The inquiry asks who represents those entities and how their integrity and evolving capacities shape decisions.

 What it opens up

Representation, access, stewardship, investment and surplus for self-owning land, buildings, infrastructure and other entities. Who speaks for them, and how are their integrity and evolving capacities upheld?
07
UniverseUniversal Basic Nutrition
 Seedling question

What infrastructure would make sufficient, healthy and ecologically sustainable nutrition ?

Exploring this term

Universal provision includes production, preparation, distribution and financing, with room for different dietary needs and cultural practices.

 What it opens up

Production, procurement, preparation, distribution and financing linked through universal provision, accommodating diverse needs and cultural practices while supporting regenerative food systems.
08
Auto-Regulating FuturesThe Future of Regulation
 Seedling question

How could become embedded in systems so they recognise their effects and adjust within collectively agreed purposes and limits?

Exploring this term

The ability to recognise effects, act on obligations and correct behaviour becomes part of how a system operates. Public purposes and the authority to revise rules remain explicit questions.

 What it opens up

Feedback, actionable obligations, correction and adaptive rules. Who sets public purposes, verifies outcomes and has the authority to revise the regulatory framework?
09
The High Frequency Feedback State
 Seedling question

How could state governance continuously sense changing conditions, learn from consequences and correct its actions through ?

Exploring this term

Different decisions need different rhythms: fast sensing, careful verification and sustained democratic deliberation, coupled to the authority and resources to respond.

 What it opens up

Relationships between public experience, evidence, democratic scrutiny, policy, budgets and delivery. Fast sensing combined with careful verification and sustained deliberation, with authority and resources to act.
10
The Future of LuxuryIn an age of austerity, violence and volatility
 Seedling question

What becomes in an age of austerity, violence and volatility?

Exploring this term

The inquiry holds open luxury as exclusive access to scarce conditions of a good life and luxury as a source of new possibilities for living well.

 What it opens up

Changing aspirations, status and privilege; exclusive access to safety, time and continuity; the legitimacy of exceptional consumption; luxury's capacity to create new possibilities and shared abundance.

The set will grow.

The invitation in year one is to work on these questions with others, surface the assumptions they carry and find where practical exploration can deepen our understanding. As new questions emerge, the set will grow.
