Draft scope · V&A Dundee with Dark Matter Labs

Scoping three living labs for the next ten years of Dundee’s development

Prepared for V&A Dundee · November 2026 · For discussion

“Scotland needs good design more than ever.” V&A Dundee, Design for Scotland

Purpose of this scope

This document sets out an initial scope for three living labs hosted by V&A Dundee and developed in partnership with Dark Matter Labs.

It follows V&A Dundee’s stated ambition to establish a lab, and its recent conversations with art schools and cultural institutions across Scotland, Wales, Ireland and England. The proposal is for a family of three labs sharing one institutional home. Each lab would hold a distinct mission and raise its own resources, while sharing staff, methods, governance and operational support.

Over a ten-year horizon, this structure would establish V&A Dundee as a working design hub for Scotland, and as a lighthouse for the wider network: a place where new practice is developed, tested and made visible to others.

Why a family of labs

Dark Matter Labs has grown as a group of labs, each with its own mission, funders and partners, supported by shared people, methods and back-office functions. The model offers three advantages over a single lab:

  • Resilience. No single funding stream carries the whole programme.
  • Focus. Each lab is accountable to a clear mission and to its own funders.
  • Flexibility. People and capability can move between labs as the work requires.

V&A Dundee brings a national mandate for design, a public building with an established visitor base, and a leading role in Scotland’s design policy through Design for Scotland. These are the conditions a family of labs needs to grow.

Lab one

Design for Scotland Lab

Purpose: to give Scotland’s public bodies access to high-quality design and strategic thinking at moments of significant decision.

Context

Design for Scotland, launched by V&A Dundee in 2022, aims to elevate design as a tool for policy development. Councils and public bodies hold significant levers over land, procurement, planning and services, yet have very little financial headroom. Public sector leaders consistently identify the lack of time for considered thinking as the greatest constraint on good decision-making.

Proposed model

A small, high-performing team working with a different public body in each sprint:

  • Core team. A standing, multidisciplinary team spanning design, systems thinking, economics and public service.
  • Fixed-term tenures. Practitioners join from partner companies, public bodies and practice for a defined period, and return with new capability.
  • Student placements. Periodic placements for students from partner art schools.
  • Sprint programme. Approximately 20 two-week sprints a year with public bodies that face a significant decision and hold the authority to act on it.
Resourcing
A pooled fund from philanthropy, with private organisations contributing staff time each year.
Partners
Local authorities, health boards, agencies and other public bodies across Scotland.
Outcomes
Better-informed public decisions and a growing network of public servants experienced in design-led practice.
Lab two

Scotland Bioregioning Lab

Purpose: to give Scotland’s bioregioning movement shared capacity to sense need, coordinate activity and share learning.

Context

Bioregioning organises action around living places, such as watersheds and firths, rather than administrative boundaries. Some of the most significant work in this field in Europe is taking place close to V&A Dundee.

  • Bioregioning Tayside ↗, a community-led initiative active since 2020, is developing the Tayside Bioregional Financing Facility ↗ with Dark Matter Labs. Work to date includes a regional stocktake, a bioregional plan, an open map of regeneration projects and trials of participatory micro-granting.
  • The Findhorn Watershed Initiative ↗ is restoring a river system from the Monadhliath Mountains to the Moray Firth.
  • RZ Studio ↗, part of the Dark Matter Labs group, supports place-based teams to build their own capital allocation capacity.

Proposed role

Bioregional work depends on what Leon Seefeld of Dark Matter Labs describes as “cascading risk sensing capabilities”, “local coordination capacity” and “comparative learning”. The lab would provide this function for Scotland: identifying emerging needs, connecting practitioners and documenting learning across bioregions. Within bioregioning practice, this is often described as a lighthouse function.

“A Bioregional Financing Facility is not built—it is grown in dialogue with place.” Leon Seefeld, They Say Art Unlocks New Perspectives ↗

Relationship with Eden Project Dundee

Eden Project Dundee is planned for the former gasworks on East Dock Street, less than a mile from V&A Dundee. Its backers have shown strong interest in bioregioning, and the visitor experience is intended to present Eden as part of the bioregion it occupies.

A bioregioning lab at V&A Dundee would allow the field, the network and the public narrative to develop before Eden breaks ground. This would establish a working relationship between the two institutions from the outset, with a clear point of handover once Eden opens.

Resourcing
Nature and place-based funders, bioregional finance, and Eden’s backers.
Partners
Bioregioning Tayside, the Findhorn watershed, and emerging bioregions across Scotland.
Outcomes
A national network able to sense, coordinate and learn, with Eden as its long-term home.
Lab three

Human Futures Lab

Purpose: to explore what it means to be human in an age shaped by AI, and how Dundee’s human capital can find new and thriving roles.

Context

Dundee’s economic history is closely tied to the skills of its people, from jute, jam and journalism to games and design. In 2014 it became the UK’s first UNESCO City of Design.

That foundation is under pressure. According to the University of Dundee, its staff headcount fell by 675 between 1 August 2024 and 1 May 2026, and further reductions have been proposed since. The effect on civic confidence extends well beyond the university. At the same time, AI is changing the nature of paid work faster than most institutions can plan for.

“What does it mean to be human now? I think this is the foundational challenge.” Indy Johar, Dark Matter Labs, on The Great Simplification ↗

In conversation with Nate Hagens, Indy Johar argues that machine capabilities mean “the human contribution to the economy as a bad robot is no longer sufficient”. He frames this as an opportunity to broaden our understanding of human contribution, including care, situated intelligence and the “freedom to be radically human”. Dundee, with deep creative reserves and a pressing need for renewal, is well placed to test these ideas in practice.

Proposed activity

  • Futures. Develop and exhibit scenarios for work, learning and care in Dundee over the next fifteen years, as a basis for public debate.
  • Prototypes. Test new roles and pathways for people leaving contracting institutions, with employers, colleges and art schools.
  • Exchange. Share findings with other cities facing similar transitions, through V&A Dundee’s institutional network.
Resourcing
Foundations focused on the future of work, learning and AI, alongside skills and regeneration funding.
Partners
Local government, colleges and universities, employers, and residents in transition.
Outcomes
A city with a renewed account of its own future, and new pathways for its people.

Shared foundations

Institutional home

The three labs share premises, operational support and a common governance framework, allowing each to remain focused while the whole remains robust.

Shared people

Tenure holders and students can work across all three labs. A public sector sprint may surface a bioregional or human futures question, and the structure allows it to be followed.

Public engagement

V&A Dundee can make the labs’ work visible through exhibitions, events and the building itself, opening it to public scrutiny and debate.

Wider network

Art schools and institutions across Scotland, Wales, Ireland and England become partners and sources of talent, extending the labs’ reach beyond Dundee.

Proposed next steps

The following steps would move this scope towards a funded programme.

  1. Scoping workshop. A half-day session in Dundee with V&A Dundee and Dark Matter Labs to test the three-lab model and agree which lab to establish first.
  2. Lab briefs. A short brief for each lab covering purpose, resourcing, partners and first-year activity, to support conversations with funders.
  3. Pilot sprint. A single two-week Design for Scotland sprint with a partner local authority, to demonstrate the model to funders and partners.
  4. Early engagement with Eden. Open the bioregioning conversation with the Eden Project team and its backers while the relationship is still forming.

Dark Matter Labs would welcome the opportunity to develop this scope further with V&A Dundee, and to share what it has learned from building labs of its own.
