Dark Matter Labs · for the team at V&A Dundee

Three labs, one house: V&A Dundee as Scotland’s design hub and lighthouse

A first sketch to start a conversation · November 2026

“Scotland needs good design more than ever.” V&A Dundee, Design for Scotland

Not one lab, but three

You want to set up a lab. Your recent run of conversations with art schools and institutions across Scotland, Wales, Ireland and England has shown how much appetite there is for it. We’d like to suggest going one step further: three labs, sharing one home at V&A Dundee, each able to raise its own money for its own kind of work.

That’s roughly how Dark Matter Labs has grown. We don’t run as one organisation with one budget. We run as a family of labs, each with its own mission, funders and partners, all sharing people, methods and a back office. A lab that has to justify itself to one funder stays small. A family of labs can follow the work, move people to where they’re needed, and survive one funding stream drying up.

V&A Dundee is unusually well placed to host this. It’s a national institution with a mandate for design, a building people already travel to, and a seat at the table of Scotland’s design policy. The three labs would let it become something more than a museum of design: a working hub where design is done for Scotland, and a lighthouse that other places steer by.

Lab one

Design for Scotland Lab

Design for Scotland already argues that design should be a tool for making policy, not just a sector of the economy. This lab would put that argument to work.

Scotland’s councils and public bodies hold real levers: land, procurement, planning, services. What they don’t have is headroom. Budgets have been cut to the bone, and the leaders we talk to say the same thing again and again: the lack of time to think properly is the biggest thing stopping them making good decisions. The work that matters most gets the least attention, because nobody has two clear weeks to give it.

So we’d give them two weeks. The lab would be a small, high-performing team that works with a different public body each sprint:

  • A standing core team from different disciplines: designers, systems thinkers, economists, people who have worked inside government.
  • Tenures, not careers. People join for a set period, from partner companies, public bodies and practice, then take what they learned back with them.
  • Students on rotation from the art schools you’ve been talking with, so the next generation learns this work by doing it.
  • About 20 two-week sprints a year, offered to councils and public bodies that have a hard decision in front of them and the power to act on it.
Powered by
A pooled fund from philanthropy, plus private firms that give staff time each year in place of cash.
Works with
Councils, health boards, agencies and other public bodies across Scotland.
Leaves behind
Better decisions, and a growing network of public servants who’ve seen what good design can do.
Lab two

Scotland Bioregioning Lab

Bioregioning is the practice of organising around a living place, a watershed or a firth, rather than lines on a map. Some of the most interesting work on it in Europe is happening on V&A Dundee’s doorstep.

Bioregioning Tayside ↗ has been building a community-led movement since 2020. With Dark Matter Labs it has been setting up the Tayside Bioregional Financing Facility ↗: a stocktake of the region, a bioregional plan, an open map of regeneration projects, and trials of community micro-granting. Further north, the Findhorn Watershed Initiative ↗ is restoring a whole river system from the Monadhliath Mountains to the Moray Firth. And RZ Studio ↗, part of the Dark Matter Labs group, is backing place-based teams like these to build their own ways of directing money.

What’s missing is something that holds it together. Bioregioning needs what Leon Seefeld at Dark Matter Labs calls “cascading risk sensing capabilities”, “local coordination capacity” and “comparative learning”: a place that notices what’s needed, connects the people doing the work, and shares what they learn. That’s the lighthouse. We think V&A Dundee could be it for Scotland.

“A Bioregional Financing Facility is not built—it is grown in dialogue with place.” Leon Seefeld, They Say Art Unlocks New Perspectives ↗

The link to Eden

Eden Project Dundee will be built on the old gasworks on East Dock Street, less than a mile from your front door. Its backers are already excited by bioregioning. The first thing a visitor will meet there is the idea that Eden is part of the bioregion it sits in, and that this is how it understands the world.

That gives V&A Dundee a rare chance. The museum can build the field, the network and the public story before Eden breaks ground, creating a strong link between the two institutions from the start. When Eden opens, there’s a handover waiting: a movement with momentum, ready to move into a building made for it.

Powered by
Nature and place-based funders, bioregional finance, and Eden’s backers.
Works with
Bioregioning Tayside, the Findhorn watershed, and emerging bioregions across Scotland.
Leaves behind
A national network that can sense, coordinate and learn, with Eden as its future home.
Lab three

Human Futures Lab

Dundee’s story has always been a story about people: jute, jam and journalism, then games and design, enough to make it the UK’s first UNESCO City of Design. Its wealth has always been the skill and imagination of the people who live there.

That story is under strain. The University of Dundee’s financial crisis has cost the city hundreds of jobs. By the university’s own account, 675 fewer people worked there in May 2026 than in August 2024, with further rounds of cuts since. That knocks the confidence of a whole city, not just a campus. At the same time, AI is changing what work people are paid to do, faster than any institution can plan for.

This lab would ask the question that sits under both: what does it mean to be human in the age we’re entering, and how does a city’s human talent find a new and thriving role? Not as a seminar topic, but as a practical inquiry with Dundee as its test bed.

“What does it mean to be human now? I think this is the foundational challenge.” Indy Johar, Dark Matter Labs, on The Great Simplification ↗

In that conversation with Nate Hagens, Indy argues that machines now mean “the human contribution to the economy as a bad robot is no longer sufficient”. He calls this an invitation to grow our idea of what being human means: care, situated intelligence, the “freedom to be radically human”. And he asks why, given everything people are capable of, “we construct economies of reducing humans to holding doors open.” Dundee, a city with deep reserves of creativity and a sharp need to reinvent itself, is the right place to try to answer that.

  • Imagine what work, learning and care could look like in Dundee in fifteen years, and show it in the galleries so the city can argue about it.
  • Prototype new roles and routes for people leaving shrinking institutions, with employers, colleges and the art schools.
  • Share it with other cities facing the same shift, through the network of institutions you’re already part of.
Powered by
Foundations working on the future of work, learning and AI, plus skills and regeneration funding.
Works with
The city, its colleges and universities, employers, and people rebuilding their working lives.
Leaves behind
A city that has taken charge of its own story, and new paths for its people.

What the three share

One house

The labs share a home, a back office and one simple set of rules, so each can stay small and focused while the whole stays sturdy.

People who move

Tenure holders and students can work across all three. A sprint with a council might turn up a bioregional question, or a human futures one.

A public face

V&A Dundee can do what few labs can: show the work. Exhibitions, events and the building itself turn the labs’ work into something the public can see and question.

A wider network

The art schools and institutions you’ve been talking with become partners and sources of talent, so the labs serve the wider network as well as Dundee.

What we’d suggest next

This is a first sketch, not a plan. We’d like to shape it with you.

  1. A working session in Dundee. Half a day with your team and ours to test the three-lab idea and decide which lab goes first.
  2. A short brief for each lab. Its purpose, who funds it, who it serves, and what a first year looks like. These are what we take to funders.
  3. One pilot sprint. Run a single two-week Design for Scotland sprint with a willing council, so funders and partners can see the model working.
  4. Bring Eden in early. Open the bioregioning conversation with the Eden team and its backers now, while the link is easy to make.

We’ve learned a lot from building labs, much of it the hard way, and we’d be glad to share all of it. Dundee has the institutions, the talent and the moment. We’d love to help build this with you.
